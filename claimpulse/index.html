Why manual denial triage fails

The bottleneck is deciding what to fight first

Claim denials are only easy to clear when someone can see the deadline, the expected recovery, and the next action before the appeal window closes. ClaimPulse makes that ordering explicit.

Manual pile

  • Deadlines get discovered too late.
  • Small and expired denials crowd out strong appeals.
  • Staff rewrite the same corrected-claim note.
  • Decisions depend on one experienced biller’s memory.

ClaimPulse queue

  • Ranked by expected recovery and deadline pressure.
  • Recommended write-offs reduce wasted effort.
  • Draft notes are generated for staff review.
  • Audit trail records who approved each draft.
How it works

Ingest, triage, draft, review

It reads the denial exports you already have, decides which deserve a fight, and hands your staff a ready-to-review next action.

1

Ingest

Drop in a denial export or paste EOB text. No EHR hookup required — it works on the claims you already handle.

2

Triage

Scores each denial: expected recovery, days left on the appeal clock, and whether it is even worth fighting.

3

Draft

Creates the next artifact — resubmission note, medical-necessity letter, or COB routing — as a draft, not a submitted claim.

4

Review

Staff approve or reject each draft. Approvals and send checks are audit-logged, and the default safety mode keeps automatic submission off.

FAQ

      
Straight answers

      
The product stops at review. Your staff stays responsible for clinical and coverage judgments.

    

    

      
Does it auto-send appeals?
No. ClaimPulse creates a draft and records a staff-review state. The local app keeps an audit trail, starts with the kill switch on, and does not submit appeals to payers.

      
Is this legal or medical advice?
No. It is a productivity copilot for billing teams and billing intermediaries. Clinical and coverage judgments stay with your staff and payers.

      
Do you touch my patients' records?
No. It runs locally on your machine against exports you already handle, and the data on this page is synthetic.

      
What do I need to start?
A CSV export of denied claims with claim number, payer, CPT, denial code, amount and appeal deadline. If you can export that, you can run a queue the same day.

      
How accurate is the triage?
It is deterministic and honest: unwinnable or expired denials are flagged as write-off instead of giving false hope. Win estimates get sharper as outcomes feed back in.
